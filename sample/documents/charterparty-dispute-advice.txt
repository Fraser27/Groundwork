Subject: Prospects on repudiatory breach and off-hire

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

In our view Northwind has a strong claim that Calder Shipping AG repudiated the time
charterparty dated 4 August 2025 by withdrawing MV Aurelia on 19 January 2026. We assess
prospects of success on liability at 70 to 75 per cent. Quantum is more uncertain and turns
on the availability of substitute tonnage in a rising market.

2. THE CHARTERPARTY

The charterparty is on an amended NYPE 2015 form. Clause 11(a) entitles the owners to
withdraw the vessel for non-payment of hire, but only after giving three banking days'
notice. Calder gave notice on 16 January 2026 and withdrew on 19 January 2026, which on any
reading is fewer than three banking days given the intervening weekend.

3. THE OFF-HIRE DISPUTE

Calder contends that hire for the period 2 to 14 January 2026 was properly due and unpaid.
Northwind withheld that hire on the basis that the vessel was off hire under clause 17
during main engine repairs at Algeciras. The engine logs disclosed on 22 March 2026 record
the main engine as inoperable from 03:40 on 2 January until 18:15 on 13 January. That is
consistent with Northwind's position and inconsistent with Calder's.

We note that the surveyor's report prepared by Halvorsen Marine reaches the opposite
conclusion, but it appears not to have had the engine logs. We consider the logs the better
evidence.

4. QUANTUM

Northwind's loss is the difference between the charter rate of USD 18,500 per day and the
market rate for substitute tonnage over the remaining 214 days of the charter. On the
Baltic Exchange assessments the market rate averaged USD 24,200 per day over the relevant
period, giving a prima facie loss of approximately USD 1.22 million before mitigation.

Calder will argue failure to mitigate. Northwind fixed the MV Sirocco on 4 February 2026,
sixteen days after withdrawal. We consider that period reasonable in a tight market, though
it is the most likely point of attack.

5. LIMITATION

The charterparty provides for LMAA arbitration in London. The claim is not time barred; the
six-year limitation period under section 5 of the Limitation Act 1980 runs from 19 January
2026.

6. RECOMMENDATION

We recommend serving a notice of arbitration before the end of April 2026 and appointing
Mr Justice-retired Alan Rowntree or Ms Frances Beaulieu as arbitrator. We further recommend
obtaining a supplementary engineering report addressing the Halvorsen conclusions directly.
