Using it

Loading the demo data

An empty LexGraph cannot demonstrate anything, because the whole point of the system is what happens when facts from different documents meet. Five matters ship with it for that reason, and they are deliberately entangled.

Getting it in

The documents are committed to the repository as sample/legal-demo.zip. Download that file, unzip it, and upload the PDFs through Documents, then Upload — the same screen a lawyer uses for anything else.

They ship as a plain zip rather than a fixture for that reason: uploading them by hand exercises the path a real user takes, so nothing about the demo is special-cased. Doing it one document at a time is also the better way to watch it, because you can stop after four and see what changes when the fifth arrives.

Set the matter on each upload

Every filename begins with its matter reference, and that is what belongs in Attach to matter: NTL-2026-0114 for the three NTL- files, MBC-2024-0431 for the facility agreement, HAL-2025-0092 for the authority note. The field accepts typed text as well as a selection, because a matter is derived from the documents filed under it and therefore has nothing to select until its first document is in.

Both things this data exists to show are cross-matter. Filing everything under one reference, or leaving it all unassigned, removes precisely what there is to look at.

An administrator can load all five at once with Admin, then Load sample data, which reads the same zip from the server. The pipeline is identical either way.

They are not seeded facts

Each page is rasterised, transcribed by a vision model, split into chunks that keep their character offsets, embedded, and read for claims. Nothing is written into the graph directly.

That distinction is the reason to load them at all. Every assertion that appears afterwards resolves to a page and a verbatim sentence on the Provenance screen, because it was genuinely read out of a document rather than declared by a fixture. A seeded demo would show the screens working and prove nothing about the system behind them.

What is in them

Three matters across five documents:

MatterDocumentWhat it puts in the graph
NTL-2026-0114 Engagement letter The client, the adverse party, and a scope exclusion — an engagement that is narrower than the client might assume.
NTL-2026-0114 Advice on prospects Reliance on an authority, The Aquitaine [2019].
NTL-2026-0114 Conflict memorandum An ethical screen, with the reason recorded.
MBC-2024-0431 Facility agreement Meridian as a client here, and a shareholder of the adverse party in the matter above.
HAL-2025-0092 Authority note The Marisol [2025] overruling The Aquitaine.

What to look for once it has loaded

Two things become true that were not true of any single document, and neither is visible to a reader of one file:

  1. A conflict. Meridian is a client in one matter and a shareholder of the adverse party in another. No document says this. It is what the graph says once both are in it, which is why conflict checking is cross-matter by definition and why tenants get one graph rather than one per matter.
  2. A stale authority. Advice in the first matter rests on The Aquitaine, and a document filed under a different matter records that it has been overruled. The advice was correct when written. That is exactly the case bitemporal fields exist to express: the world changed, not the record of it.

Both facts arrive as INFERRED, so each carries the proof tree that produced it. Follow it and you reach the two documents and the sentences in them, which is the answer to why does the system believe this in its concrete form.

A question worth asking first

Ask "are there any conflicts on the Northwind matter?" before loading the fifth document, then again afterwards. The answer changes, and the citation shows which document changed it.

The other half: practice data in Athena

The documents are only half the demo. LexGraph governs structured data too, and the questions worth asking are the ones that need both — who is the adverse party comes from a page, what have we billed them comes from a warehouse. Until there are rows somewhere, half the product has nothing to stand on.

./sample/glue/load_firm_metadata.sh

That creates a Glue database called lexgraph_legal with three Iceberg tables — clients, matters and time_entries — holding ten clients, ten matters and twenty-four time entries. Then in Admin, Scan catalog, tick lexgraph_legal. Only the schema enters the graph; the rows stay in S3 and are queried in place.

The data is not arbitrary. It uses the names, references, partners and charge-out rates the PDFs actually contain: NTL-2026-0114 is led by Perrine Duval and opened 12 March 2026 in both places, and the rates are the ones the engagement letter quotes. That correlation is the point, because a row and an extracted fact that describe the same engagement can be joined, and two that differ by a character cannot.

The most interesting row is the one that is missing

Calder Shipping AG is deliberately absent from clients, because the conflict memorandum states the firm has never acted for it. It appears only as the adverse party on NTL-2026-0114. So "is Calder Shipping a client of ours?" returns no rows, and that emptiness is the conflict analysis being confirmed rather than a gap in the data — Meridian is a client and a shareholder in Calder; Calder itself is not a client. Adding a row for Calder would contradict the document and quietly make the whole demo incoherent.

Matters live in sample/glue/matters.csv rather than inside the script, because the same ten records are also the matters you file documents under. One list, read twice.

Re-running the script converges instead of duplicating: the tables are Iceberg, so it clears each one and reloads it. That is the reason for Iceberg here rather than a crawler over CSV — a demo you cannot re-run without clearing S3 by hand is a demo people stop running.

Starting again

Admin, then Reset takes the derived layers away: the graph, the search index, ingest job history, and the cached catalog schemas. It ticks what to remove, so a wipe can be partial.

None of that touches S3. The bucket is the source of truth, so Replay rebuilds every document from it and Scan catalog rebuilds the schemas from Glue. A bad extraction run is a re-run, not a data-loss event.

Metric definitions are the exception

They are kept by default, and deleting them needs the box ticked and a separate confirmation. Everything else in a reset has an upstream source that can regenerate it; a metric was authored by a person in the application and has none. Nothing rebuilds it, so the warning says so in those words rather than asking "are you sure".

Changing what they say

The PDFs are generated by sample/generate_demo_pdfs.py, and the zip it produces is committed so that downloading the demo needs nothing installed. Edit the script and re-run it to change what the demo asserts.

.venv/bin/python sample/generate_demo_pdfs.py

Keep the matter reference at the front of each filename. That is where the matter is read from, so a document added to the zip needs no code change.
