Using it

Loading the demo data

An empty LexGraph cannot demonstrate anything, because the whole point of the system is what happens when facts from different documents meet. Five matters ship with it for that reason, and they are deliberately entangled.

Loading it

Sign in as an administrator and use Admin, then Load sample data. The documents are PDFs, committed to the repository as sample/legal-demo.zip so that a fresh checkout needs no PDF toolchain to run the demo.

They are not seeded facts

The files are uploaded to the document bucket and then go through the same pipeline as anything a lawyer uploads: each page is rasterised, transcribed by a vision model, split into chunks that keep their character offsets, embedded, and read for claims. Nothing is written into the graph directly.

That distinction is the reason to load them at all. Every assertion that appears afterwards resolves to a page and a verbatim sentence on the Provenance screen, because it was genuinely read out of a document rather than declared by a fixture. A seeded demo would show the screens working and prove nothing about the system behind them.

What is in them

Three matters across five documents:

MatterDocumentWhat it puts in the graph
NTL-2026-0114 Engagement letter The client, the adverse party, and a scope exclusion — an engagement that is narrower than the client might assume.
NTL-2026-0114 Advice on prospects Reliance on an authority, The Aquitaine [2019].
NTL-2026-0114 Conflict memorandum An ethical screen, with the reason recorded.
MBC-2024-0431 Facility agreement Meridian as a client here, and a shareholder of the adverse party in the matter above.
HAL-2025-0092 Authority note The Marisol [2025] overruling The Aquitaine.

What to look for once it has loaded

Two things become true that were not true of any single document, and neither is visible to a reader of one file:

  1. A conflict. Meridian is a client in one matter and a shareholder of the adverse party in another. No document says this. It is what the graph says once both are in it, which is why conflict checking is cross-matter by definition and why tenants get one graph rather than one per matter.
  2. A stale authority. Advice in the first matter rests on The Aquitaine, and a document filed under a different matter records that it has been overruled. The advice was correct when written. That is exactly the case bitemporal fields exist to express: the world changed, not the record of it.

Both facts arrive as INFERRED, so each carries the proof tree that produced it. Follow it and you reach the two documents and the sentences in them, which is the answer to why does the system believe this in its concrete form.

A question worth asking first

Ask "are there any conflicts on the Northern Trade matter?" before loading the fifth document, then again afterwards. The answer changes, and the citation shows which document changed it.

Starting again

Admin, then Reset takes the derived layers away: the graph, the search index, ingest job history, and the cached catalog schemas. It ticks what to remove, so a wipe can be partial.

None of that touches S3. The bucket is the source of truth, so Replay rebuilds every document from it and Scan catalog rebuilds the schemas from Glue. A bad extraction run is a re-run, not a data-loss event.

Metric definitions are the exception

They are kept by default, and deleting them needs the box ticked and a separate confirmation. Everything else in a reset has an upstream source that can regenerate it; a metric was authored by a person in the application and has none. Nothing rebuilds it, so the warning says so in those words rather than asking "are you sure".

Over the API

curl -X POST -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" \
  "$BASE/api/tenants/$TENANT/admin/sample-data"

The PDFs are generated by sample/generate_demo_pdfs.py and the zip is committed. Edit the script and re-run it to change what the demo asserts.
